Olive oil has been valued for thousands of years and was once known as "liquid gold" in ancient civilizations. The olive tree became a symbol of peace, prosperity, and longevity throughout the Mediterranean region.

Τὰ ὀφέλη · Benefits

One of nature's most valued foods

Celebrated for both its exceptional taste and nutritional qualities, extra virgin olive oil is naturally rich in monounsaturated fats, antioxidants, and polyphenols.

Α

Rich in Polyphenols

Polyphenols are natural antioxidants found in olives. They protect the oil's freshness, flavor, and stability, and give high-quality extra virgin olive oil its distinctive taste.

Β

Crafted by Nature

From carefully selected olives to gentle extraction methods, every bottle reflects the harmony of tradition, craftsmanship, and nature. The result is exceptional flavor in every drop.

Στὴν κουζίνα · In the Kitchen

Made for frying, too

Extra virgin olive oil brings Mediterranean flavour to the pan as well as the table. Use the cooking guide below as a starting point, adjusting the heat to your recipe and ingredients. Avoid overheating or letting the oil smoke.

HeatTemperatureBest for
Medium130–145 °CFoods high in water: vegetables, potatoes, fruit
Hot155–170 °CFoods coated in batter, flour or breadcrumbs
Very hot175–190 °CSmall, quick-fried foods: small fish, croquettes
  • Always add food to hot oil; cold oil gets soaked up.
  • Use plenty of oil when deep frying for even cooking.
  • Don't mix olive oil with other fats.

Κρητικὴ διατροφή · The Cretan Diet

The Mediterranean table

The traditional Cretan table is simple, seasonal, and full of flavour. At its heart is olive oil, alongside bread and grains, pulses, vegetables and fruit, with cheese and fish in modest amounts.

  • Use olive oil as your main fat.
  • Build meals around vegetables, fruit, grains, beans, nuts and seeds.
  • Choose fresh, seasonal, minimally processed food.
  • Enjoy cheese and yoghurt daily in modest amounts, and fish and poultry weekly.
  • Finish with fresh fruit.
